The problem

Indian real estate is riddled
with hidden legal risks

Most buyers discover problems only after signing. By then, it's too late — and the financial and legal consequences can be devastating.

Title fraud

Forged sale deeds, fake ownership documents, and impersonation of sellers are alarmingly common in resale markets across India.

Unclear ownership

Multiple claimants, undivided family property disputes, and incomplete transfer chains leave buyers exposed to ownership challenges years later.

Active legal disputes

Properties under litigation, court attachments, or government acquisition proceedings are often sold without disclosure to unsuspecting buyers.

Hidden encumbrances

Undisclosed mortgages, bank loans, and liens on the property can transfer to the new buyer, creating unexpected financial liabilities.

Document inconsistencies

Mismatched names, incorrect survey numbers, missing approvals, and altered documents are red flags that go unnoticed without expert review.

Unapproved construction

Buildings without proper approvals, RERA registration, or occupancy certificates expose buyers to demolition orders and legal complications.

Over ₹1.2 lakh crore in property disputes are pending in Indian courts. Most could have been avoided with proper due diligence.
